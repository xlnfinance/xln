# Questions and answers

**[← Index](../readme.md)** | **[Next: UFT →](10_UFT.md)**

## Author and motivation

Egor Homakov is the author of UFT and xln. His work began in software engineering
and security research, including the Rails mass-assignment issue, then boutique
security work at [Sakurity](https://sakurity.com). Since 2017 his focus has been the
architecture of financial systems. UFT/xln draws on nine years of research into
settlement, bilateral accounts, organizational authority and programmable finance.

## What is the idea?

Existing finance already has jurisdictions, entities and accounts. xln makes
those financial relationships replicable, verifiable and signed, with account
proofs that can be exercised in an underlying programmable J-machine.

[UFT](10_UFT.md) is the model, [RCPAN](12_invariant.md) the account bound, and
xln the implementation. The ambition is to make existing economic activity
provable, rather than create a separate financial universe.

## What does MML mean?

By 2050, make accounts supporting 51% of world GDP provable and disputable on
programmable Js. J reserves are part of that financial foundation too; account
claims and their backing must be reconciled without counting the same value twice.
See [the mission](../intro.md#mission).

## Does credit undermine the proof?

No. Proof establishes the agreed obligation; collateral backs its secured portion.
The grantor chooses unsecured exposure, manually or through soft-limit policy.
Zero credit is valid. A proof of debt does not guarantee that an insolvent debtor
can repay it. Credit selection belongs to the parties and operators, while the
runtime enforces the agreed limits before signing.

## What protects a user if a hub fails?

Three distinct protections: retained signed evidence for dispute, chosen collateral
backing, and Delta Transformers enforcing conditions while value moves. A user can
claim the secured entitlement under J's rules; any remaining unsecured receivable
follows the actual reserve/debt enforcement path.

This improves control over financial claims and bounds direct account exposure.
It does not make every hub solvent, eliminate shared-operator failures or remove
all first-mover incentives on unsecured reserves.

## Why not globally publish every financial operation?

Unrelated parties do not need an account's history to agree on its next state.
Independent account updates can progress locally, while J handles registration,
collateral, net settlement and disputes. The parties or delegates retain the
usable proof. This changes ordinary agreement and evidence scope; it does not
remove the chosen J's own consensus, availability and inclusion requirements.

## Can bilateral accounts support programmable financial services?

Yes. Payments, swaps, lending and conditional orders are part of the architecture.
A pool, auction or clearing house can be an Entity with bilateral financial
relationships. Coordination inside that service does not require the entire
economy to share one execution domain. Comparing implementations still requires
preserving the same pricing, rights, liquidity and failure outcomes.

## Which jurisdictions and which small-device verifier?

Ethereum, TRON and XLNC are the initial focus; Base, Arbitrum and other compatible
EVM Js can share the same financial path after their boundaries are verified.
The small-device full-verification objective targets **XLNC**, pronounced
“excellence”, a compact J tailored to xln. It does not mean storing and executing
the full Ethereum or TRON history on a watch.

Expensive ordinary J activity encourages the Account layer. XLNC is a conventional
stateful EVM J with roughly 10–20 times less block gas capacity, not a stateless
or ZK design. Phones and laptops run ordinary full nodes and synchronize normally.
Those resource budgets and emergency-dispute capacity need measurements. See
[XLNC design](../xlnc-soft-mainnet.md).

## Is one hour the complete dispute duration?

The intended default is one hour for a hub to react to a user-started dispute.
Cross-J settlement additionally needs enough time to observe and copy valid lock/
reveal evidence. Current code sums signed hub/user windows, yielding 25 hours
with the default one-hour/24-hour policy. A responder-only reaction rule and its
safe finalization conditions must be distinguished before changing that code.

## What should I try first?

Fund an account, pay another user, swap, withdraw and recover after hub failure.
The [wallet acceptance plan](../wallet-journey-plan.md) records the new-UI walkthrough
and existing-frontend E2E requirements. Release readiness is established by actual
candidate evidence, not an architectural argument or an old launch date.

## Has billion-TPS or universal superiority been demonstrated?

No billion-TPS production measurement is established here. The architectural
claim is horizontal capacity across independent accounts, with low ordinary J
load. Routing capacity, concentrated hubs and simultaneous disputes remain real
constraints. [The comparison](../competitors.md) distinguishes that contribution
from individual prior ideas and from unmeasured product claims.
