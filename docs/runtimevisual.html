Runtime floorplan — one frame, three stages, N workers

Every Account input carries an id. Its first 12 bits pick one of 4096 logical shards; the shard picks a worker and keeps it for the life of the process. Watch inputs land, re-bucket at each barrier into Paybook and Orderbook, come back into the same workers as proposals, seal one root, hit the disk, and only then reach the network. docs/runtime.md

Runtime poisoned — WAL write failed at frame —

Frame N was not published. Speculative frame N+1 is discarded, later frames are not attempted, and apply is never retried against uncertain in-memory state. Recovery starts from the last durable authority: one canonical checkpoint plus the ordered Runtime WAL, replayed through the same production transitions. Reseed restarts the machine.

One pool, every shard family

Each worker permanently owns a slice of Account, Paybook, Orderbook and Crontab shards — the four dots beside its label. Separate per-module pools are forbidden precisely because they idle: a Paybook pool would sleep through both Account stages.

The barrier is a re-bucketing

At JOIN 1 the Account effects are re-keyed by hashlock and pair and land in whichever worker owns that book shard — the chords drawn across the lanes. At JOIN 2 the intents are re-keyed by accountId and come back into the workers as stage-3 proposals.

Mutation is not commitment

Stages 1–3 invalidate paths; they never hash them. Only the seal asks for roots, and only for distinct invalid nodes — O(dirty), never O(state). Clean subtrees reuse their cached commitment, which is why that third counter stays at zero.

Refusal beats an unbounded queue

The mempool is finite and lives only in RAM — it is Replica state, never committed state. Offer more than the pool can finish and the Runtime refuses the excess at the door rather than growing latency without limit. Drop the worker count while the load is high and watch the refusal rate become the whole story.

Durability is a gate, not a hint

The committer is the only other thread, and it is I/O-bound by definition. The outbox opens after fsync returns, never before. Raise the fsync slider and the WAL becomes the wall — visible as red blocking segments, not as lost frames.
