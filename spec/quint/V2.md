# v2 proposals: order book, lending, boards

These are proposals, not models. v1 (payments, HTLC routing, disputes, settlement, batch, runtime) is specified and checked; v2 is the
list of what has to be true before the next product can be built on it, in the order I would build it. Each proposal says what state
it adds, what transitions, which properties the checker must have, what it asks of the contracts, and what is open. Sources: `docs/lend.md`,
`docs/lending-review-2026-09-07.md`, `core/types/account.ts` (SwapOffer, swap_resolve), `core/orderbook/`, `contracts/contracts/`
(`DeltaTransformer.sol`, `EntityProvider.sol`), plan/contracts-review.md (H3). og is the reference for what was built, never the oracle
for what is right.

## Order of work (my recommendation)

1. **Several tokens per Account.** Everything below needs it, and v1 is single-token on purpose. It touches every layer.
2. **Swaps and the order book.** The most self-contained: a clause type that the chain already knows how to read.
3. **Lending.** It adds time and default, and the docs themselves list the open questions (term-claim enforcement, principal accounting,
   approval authority, default).
4. **Boards.** Independent of the others; the hardest to get right, and v1 only needs a board of one.

## 0. Prerequisite: several tokens

- Body: `offdelta`, `limitLeft`, `limitRight` become maps over tokens; the chain reads `ondelta` and `collateral` per token. RCPAN holds per token,
  in the worst case over the open clauses of that token.
- Chain: `payout` runs per token; the shortfall and debt rule (`enforce`, `shortfall`) are per token already in the contract
  (`debtOutstanding[entity][token]`).
- Batch: `MAX_ENCODED_BATCH_BYTES` and the per-array bounds start to matter; a full batch is a refusal (R-J3).
- Properties carried over: P1 to P4 per token; P3 conserved per token. New: no cross-token leak (an offdelta of one token never changes what a proof
  pays in another).
- Model change: `Body.offdelta: int` becomes `int -> int` over `TOKENS = Set(1, 2)`; `chain.qnt` gets `money`, `coll`, `ondelta` per token. A
  mechanical change; the mutants of v1 carry over per token.

## 1. Swaps and the order book

**What og built.** A maker's limit order is a `SwapOffer` in its Account with the hub (give token and amount, want token and amount, limit
price in ticks, time in force GTC/IOC/FOK, fee bounds). The hub matches offers across its Accounts and resolves each with `swap_resolve`
(a fill ratio, cancel the remainder or keep it). On chain the offer is a clause whose outcome depends on a fill ratio the counterparty
supplies as dispute evidence.

**Proposed state.** A swap clause in the Body next to the HTLC clauses: `{id, maker, giveToken, wantToken, giveAmount, wantAmount, limit
price, expires}`. It holds the maker's `giveAmount` in RCPAN like an HTLC lock holds an amount, and the counterparty's want-side capacity
too (both tokens): a swap that the taker cannot cover is refused when it is offered, not when it is filled.

**Proposed transitions.** `SwapOffer`, `SwapFill(id, ratio)` (partial: the remainder stays open with the same price), `SwapCancel(id)`
(the maker asks, the counterparty resolves, as in og), `SwapExpire(id)` (after `expires`, anyone). A fill changes both tokens' offdelta by
the exact amounts of `ratio` in one frame.

**Properties.** (1) A fill never moves a price past the limit: `receive / give >= limit` for every fill. (2) Conservation: the two tokens'
offdeltas move by amounts that satisfy the price, nothing is created (checked per token: P3). (3) Rounding never favours the taker over the
maker by more than one lot, and the residual stays open, never negative (og's `LOT_SCALE` quantization is the risk here). (4) Price-time
priority at the hub is a property of the matcher, not of the Account: two orders at one price fill in arrival order. (5) In a dispute the
proof pays the last co-signed fill state, and an open swap with no fill evidence pays as unfilled (same rule as H1 for an unrevealed HTLC:
a finalize waits for the swap's expiry when the fill is the counterparty's to show).

**Between Accounts.** A fill on the taker's Account and a fill on the maker's Account are two frames in two Accounts: the hub holds a
position while one is done and the other not. The safe order is the one the routing model already has for HTLCs: lock both sides first
(each side's clause holds the amount), then resolve; a fill that fails on the second leg is undone by the expiry of the first. That is
the entity-level property to check: the hub's net position across the two tokens is zero after every complete fill and bounded by one
open leg otherwise.

**Asks of the contracts.** The swap clause with a fill ratio as evidence exists (`DeltaTransformer`); it needs the same H1 treatment as the HTLC
(a finalize over an open swap waits) and a check that a ratio can only go up in evidence. The contracts thread should confirm both.

**Open.** Fees (per fill, per token, who pays: og has `maxFee` and `minNetReceive`); cross-jurisdiction routes (`CrossJurisdictionSwapRoute`),
which I would leave out of v2.

## 2. Lending

**What the docs say.** A hub pool per token; lend offers and borrow requests with a term (1 hour, 1 day, 1 month), a rate, principal, maturity;
positions are bilateral facts in the user's Account with the hub; the hub approves borrowers by hand; repayment closes the position.

**Proposed state.** A term clause, not an HTLC: `{id, lender, borrower, token, principal, interestDue, opensAt, maturesAt}`. Principal is
locked in the lender's side of the Account for the term (the same accounting as a lock: it counts against the lender's room). At maturity
the borrower owes principal + interest.

**Proposed transitions.** `LoanOpen` (accepted quote, both sign), `Repay(id)` (before or at maturity: principal + interest move back and
the clause closes), `Default(id)` (after maturity: the clause resolves against the borrower's collateral, or becomes debt).

**The questions the docs leave open, with my choice for each.**
1. Term-claim enforcement: what makes the lender's claim real on chain? A loan is only as good as the borrower's collateral in the Account. The
   clause must be covered when it is opened: `limit` and collateral of the borrower's side at least `principal + interestDue` (the same RCPAN
   check, now with a time horizon). A loan the borrower cannot cover is refused at open.
2. Principal accounting: the principal is a transfer at open and returns at repayment; the clause holds the *claim* `principal + interest`
   against the borrower's side from open to maturity. Property: the claim is inside RCPAN at every height until repaid or defaulted.
3. Approval authority: a hub decision is an Entity command (signed, atomic, nonce-bound, E8); a user cannot open a loan the hub did not approve, and
   the approval is part of the frame both sign.
4. Default: at maturity with the claim unpaid, the chain pays from the borrower's share and books the rest as debt (`enforce`, `shortfall`, already
   in chain.qnt). Nothing new is needed on the contract if the term clause settles like an HTLC that pays the lender at `maturesAt`. Property: a
   default never pays more than the claim.

**Properties.** No loan opens outside RCPAN; conservation per token; interest accrues linearly and never negative; a repayment before
maturity pays exactly `principal + interest(t)`; after maturity only the default path exists (no repay race with a finalize).

**Asks of the contracts.** A term clause type (or the HTLC clause with a time-based release instead of a hash), payout rule unchanged.

**Open.** Variable rates, liquidation, secondary trading: out of scope, as the docs say.

## 3. Boards

**What exists.** An Entity is a board of validators with a threshold; a hanko is the board's signature; `EntityProvider` verifies a hanko
against the entity's *current* board; the board rotates by proposal. H3 (plan/contracts-review.md): a retired board keeps seven days of dispute
evidence with no cap, a trade-off, not a fix.

**Proposed model.** A small BFT: a leader proposes an Entity frame, validators sign, a quorum of signatures commits it; a timeout rotates the
leader (view change); a board rotation is a frame like any other, effective at a height.

**Properties.** (1) Agreement: no two frames commit at one height (holds while fewer than the threshold are faulty). (2) A view change
never halts anyone: og's B-E1 (the superseded proposer halts on `ENTITY_VALIDATOR_EXECUTION_FRAME_MISMATCH`) and B-E2 (a J-prefix retry is never
re-proposed, so the board stalls) are the two bugs this must make impossible. (3) Every J event range has one owner across leader rotation (Q-E3):
the range a timed-out leader held is either committed by it or re-proposed by the next leader, never both, never neither. (4) A rotation takes
effect at one height on every honest validator, and evidence signed by the retired board is accepted only inside the H3 window.

**Asks of the contracts.** The seven-day window of H3 as a decision (a constant, capped or not), and the rule for what a retired board can still
sign (evidence of a state it co-signed, not a new one).

**Open.** Whether the board layer is v2 at all: the chain-level flaws (C1, C2, H1, H2) were closed without it, and the Runtime layer treats a board
of one as the base case. The model above is worth writing when a board of more than one is a product requirement.
